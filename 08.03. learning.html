How to use this hub

  • Flash cards: click a card (or press the space bar) to flip it; use the arrows or the ← → keys to move; filter by lesson; shuffle when the order becomes familiar.
  • Quiz: one question at a time. An MCQ (Multiple-Choice Question) has four options and one answer — click it. An MSQ (Multiple-Select Question) has six options and the number of expected answers in round brackets, for example "(select 3)" — tick that many, then check.
  • Every option carries its reason after you answer — read the reasons of the wrong options too; they are the traps of the exam.
  • At the end: your score, a breakdown by lesson and by question type, and a review list of the questions to look at again; "Retry the missed questions" repeats only those.

What to have in mind before the quiz

  • Evidence first: events, metrics, flow records, packets, states — each points to a category.
  • Same category = interchangeable tools; different categories = complementary.
  • The need comes first; the tool comes last; knock-outs before scores; weights before any score.
  • Every tool named in Class 8 is lawful in your lab on Designated Targets and an offence elsewhere — section 7.2.

What this hub covers

Lesson 3.1 — Categories of monitoring tools: what a monitoring tool is (three functions, four components); the five kinds of evidence; the seven categories C1 to C7 with their limits and their placement on the Val-des-Pins network; the six classification axes; the coverage map and the Lab 2 result (Tier 1 needs C1 + C3 + C5; gap on CNC-PC-01).

Lesson 3.2 — Selecting the right tool for a given need: the six elements of a testable need; requirements, preferences, knock-outs and weighted criteria; the constraints that filter the list; the seven-step procedure and the weighted total formula; frequent errors; the four mini-cases; the Val-des-Pins decision matrix (Wazuh 89.0 %, Graylog Open 69.0 %).

Companion materials: the Class 8 lecture page (professor edition and student handout), the student deck (63 slides) and the reference tables. Quiz 2 (5 %) is a separate document written at the end of the class.
