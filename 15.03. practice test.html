MCQQuestion 1module section 5.1.1

Which statement gives the three parts that every definition of an anomaly needs?

  • Wrong: these are products, not the parts of the definition.
  • Correct: baseline, deviation, tolerance — the structure of lesson 5.1.1 and of Example 5.1.
  • Wrong: these are signatures (known bad patterns), used by one detection method only.
  • Wrong: an anomaly has a cause, which is not necessarily an attacker.
Hint: Remove one of the three and the word "anomaly" has no meaning.

One user logs on to SRV-AD01 at 09:02. In the vocabulary of the lesson, that occurrence is:

  • Wrong: it becomes an anomaly only if it deviates from the baseline beyond the tolerance.
  • Wrong: an incident requires a violation of security policy.

MCQQuestion 3module section 5.1.2

A port scan of srv-web01 from a workstation is best described as which kind of sign?

  • Correct: a scan is the classic precursor; the attacker maps the target before acting.
  • Wrong: an indicator points to an incident that may already have occurred (a success after many failures).
  • Wrong: a false negative is unwanted activity that was not flagged.
  • Wrong: unless the scan is documented and authorised, it is not benign.
Hint: Scanning is mapping before acting.
MCQQuestion 4module section 5.1.3

A VPN logon by the account backup-svc, which never uses VPN, is which family of anomaly?

  • Wrong: a point anomaly is a value far outside the band, whatever the context.
  • Correct: an ordinary value in a context in which it is not expected is a contextual anomaly.
  • Wrong: a collective anomaly is a set or sequence of ordinary observations.
  • Wrong: "signature" is a detection method, not an anomaly family.
Hint: The value (one logon) is ordinary; the context (this account, this channel) is not.
MCQQuestion 5module section 5.1.5

The baseline of a metric is mean 60 and standard deviation 15. The observation is 98. The z-score is:

  • Wrong: 38 / 60 — dividing by the mean, not by σ.
  • Correct: (98 − 60) / 15 = 2.53, inside a 3-sigma tolerance.
  • Wrong: that would need σ = 10.
  • Wrong: 38 is the difference, not the z-score.
Hint: z = (98 − 60) / 15.
MCQQuestion 6module section 5.1.5

Which detection method compares each record with a list of known bad patterns such as file hashes or domain names?

  • Wrong: threshold-based compares a count with a fixed number.
  • Wrong: statistical uses the mean and standard deviation of the baseline.
  • Correct: that is the definition of signature-based detection (NIST SP 800-94).
  • Wrong: behavioural keeps a profile per entity and context.
Hint: Precise for known threats, blind to new ones.
MCQQuestion 7module section 5.1.6

A rule produces a false-positive rate of 1 % on a network that records 20,000 events a day. About how many false alarms a day does it generate?

  • Wrong: that would be 0.01 %.
  • Wrong: that would be 0.1 %.
  • Correct: 1 % of 20,000 = 200 — far more than the number of real incidents, which is why triage exists.
  • Wrong: that would be 10 %.
Hint: Base-rate effect: 1 % of 20,000.
MCQQuestion 8module section 5.1.7

On November 1 the clocks change (end of daylight-saving time). What is the effect on an hour-of-day baseline that is not adjusted?

  • Wrong: a local-time baseline is common, and even a UTC baseline describes activity that follows local working hours.
  • Correct: the 09:00 profile now applies to what is really 08:00 activity; every host looks abnormal at once.
  • Wrong: the sign of z depends on the direction of the deviation, not on the clock change.
  • Wrong: the shift creates contextual anomalies (ordinary values at the wrong hour).
Hint: A calendar effect from section 5.1.7.
MCQQuestion 9module section 5.2.1

"An alert is a claim, not a fact." What does the sentence mean for the technician?

  • Wrong: ignoring alerts is the alert-fatigue failure of 5.2.8.

  • Wrong: severity is a value attached to the rule, not a proof.
  • Wrong: only P1 decisions escalate immediately, after triage.

MCQQuestion 11module section 5.2.3

MCQQuestion 12module section 5.2.5

Alert: rule severity 3, asset criticality 2, account "ops" (privileged). Using the impact scale of Example 5.2, the impact is:

  • Wrong: the asset criticality alone ignores the rule severity.
  • Wrong: the privileged account adds one level.
  • Correct: max(3, 2) = 3, plus 1 for the privileged account = 4.
  • Wrong: 5 would need a critical asset or a severity-5 rule.
Hint: max(rule severity, asset criticality) + 1 for a privileged account, capped at 5.
MCQQuestion 13module section 5.2.5

Why does the method multiply impact by confidence instead of adding them?

  • Wrong: the size of the numbers is irrelevant; the ordering is what matters.
  • Correct: a certain alert on a trivial asset and an uncertain alert on a critical asset both score low; only "serious AND credible" scores high.
  • Wrong: NIST describes the factors; the arithmetic is a practical choice of this course.
  • Wrong: benign alerts receive confidence 1 and therefore score low.
Hint: Think of impact 5 × confidence 1 versus impact 1 × confidence 5.
